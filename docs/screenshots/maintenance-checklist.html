Honda Civic · 2018 · 100,000 km

This is general guidance based on typical maintenance intervals, not a substitute for a qualified mechanic's inspection. General educational information only — not professional mechanical advice. Independent proof of concept — not affiliated with PakWheels.

Basics

  • Review manufacturer service schedule rule

    Every vehicle has model-specific intervals in the handbook or workshop manual.

  • Confirm last oil & filter change date rule

    Fresh oil is the most common routine service; records help verify upkeep.

Engine

  • Engine oil & filter change interval rule

    Typical routine service around 5,000–10,000 km depending on oil type and driving.

  • Spark plug condition (petrol) rule

    Worn plugs can cause misfires and poor fuel average on petrol engines.

  • Timing belt / chain inspection rule

    Many engines use a timing belt that must be replaced at a set interval — verify for your exact engine variant with a workshop.

  • Drive belts and tensioners rule

    Cracked or glazed belts can fail suddenly; inspect at major service milestones.

  • Engine mount condition rule

    Excess vibration or thumping under load can indicate worn mounts.

Filters

  • Air filter inspection / replacement rule

    Clogged air filters reduce performance and fuel economy.

Brakes

  • Brake pad and disc inspection rule

    Wear increases stopping distance; inspect before long trips.

  • Full brake system review (pads, discs, lines) rule

    Higher mileage cars often need more than a quick pad glance.

Fluids

  • Brake fluid moisture / level check rule

    Old brake fluid can absorb moisture and reduce braking effectiveness.

Transmission

  • Transmission / gearbox fluid check rule

    Fluid condition matters for smooth shifts in automatic and manual cars.

  • Clutch wear check (manual transmission) rule

    Slipping or high bite point may indicate clutch replacement is due.

Cooling

  • Coolant level and hose inspection rule

    Cooling system issues can lead to overheating in hot climates.

Suspension

  • Suspension bushings and shock absorbers rule

    High mileage often brings worn bushings or weak dampers affecting ride and tyre wear.

Electrical

  • Battery health test rule

    Batteries typically weaken after several years, especially in heat.

Underbody

  • Rust / underbody inspection rule

    Older vehicles in humid or coastal areas benefit from periodic underbody checks.

Tyres

  • Tyre age and tread depth rule

    Rubber hardens with age even if tread looks acceptable.

Related knowledge

  • Timing belt and major engine service similarity 78%

    Some petrol engines use a rubber timing belt that must be replaced at a manufacturer interval (often between roughly 60,000-100,000 km depending on engine). Verify the exact variant with a workshop or handbook.

  • Brake system maintenance similarity 71%

    Brake pads and discs wear with use. Squealing, longer stopping distances, or a soft pedal are reasons to have brakes inspected by a qualified mechanic.
