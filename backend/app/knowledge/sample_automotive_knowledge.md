## Checking a used car before you buy

When considering a used car in Pakistan, buyers commonly review documents, condition, and a short test drive. Typical checks include matching the chassis number on documents with the car, reviewing registration papers, asking about owners and accidents, looking for uneven panel gaps or mismatched paint, listening for unusual engine or transmission noises, and verifying that lights, AC, and electrics work. A trusted mechanic inspection is often recommended before payment. This is general educational information, not a substitute for a professional inspection.

## Ownership and documents

Important paperwork often includes the registration book, computerized registration details where applicable, token tax status, and clarity about outstanding bank finance or hypothecation. Buyers usually ask whether the seller is the registered owner and how many previous owners the car had. Missing or inconsistent documents are a reason to pause and verify before transferring money. AutoAI cannot verify documents for you — treat document checks as something you or a trusted helper must do in person.

## What resale value depends on

Resale value in Pakistan is influenced by brand and model demand, model year, mileage, overall condition, accident and paint history, service records, transmission type, and local market preferences. Popular models with strong parts availability often hold value better than rare or poorly supported ones. High mileage, unresolved mechanical issues, or unclear ownership history can reduce what the next buyer will pay. Resale guidance here is educational only and not a formal valuation.

## Common maintenance intervals (general)

As a general educational guide, many petrol cars follow service patterns such as oil and filter changes on a regular kilometre or time interval recommended by the manufacturer, periodic air filter and cabin filter checks, brake inspection when pedal feel changes, and coolant or transmission fluid service according to the handbook. Exact intervals vary by make, model, driving style, and climate. Always prefer the vehicle handbook or a qualified workshop over generic rules of thumb.

## Manual vs automatic vs CVT

A manual transmission uses a clutch pedal and gear stick; the driver selects gears. A conventional automatic typically changes gears without a clutch pedal and can be easier in city traffic. A CVT (continuously variable transmission) uses a belt or pulley system to vary ratios smoothly rather than fixed gears, which can feel different under acceleration and is common on some modern cars. Suitability depends on driving preference, maintenance access, and repair cost expectations. This is terminology education, not a recommendation for a specific model.

## What engine capacity means

Engine capacity (often stated in cc, such as 1300cc or 1800cc) describes the total volume of the engine's cylinders. In everyday buying conversations it is used as a rough signal of power and sometimes fuel use, but real fuel economy and performance also depend on vehicle weight, transmission, driving style, traffic, and maintenance. A larger engine is not automatically better for every buyer. Treat capacity as one specification among many, not a guarantee of economy or reliability.

## Fuel average and running costs

Fuel average (km per litre) is often discussed when budgeting running costs. Advertised or claimed averages can differ from real-world city driving. Factors include traffic, AC use, tyre condition, and driving habits. Hybrid and electric options have different cost profiles. Use fuel figures as approximate planning aids, not guarantees. ## Accident history and paint

Accident history and whether paint is original are common questions because repairs can affect safety and resale. A seller claim that a car is accident-free is not proof. Buyers often look for paint thickness differences, panel alignment, and underbody or structural clues, and may seek an independent inspection. Never treat listing text alone as confirmation that a car is accident-free or mechanically perfect.

## Token tax and registration basics

In Pakistan, buyers commonly confirm that token tax and related dues are paid up to date and that registration details match the vehicle. Procedures and portals can change over time, so verify current requirements with official sources or a trusted registration helper. This knowledge base gives only a high-level overview and is not legal advice.

## Why sellers sell and negotiation

Asking why the seller is selling can surface useful context (relocation, upgrading, cash need), but answers should be cross-checked with condition and documents. Negotiation often considers market comps, remaining work needed, and paperwork clarity. Educational tip only — AutoAI does not set prices or negotiate on your behalf.

## Oil change and filter basics

Engine oil and filter changes are among the most common routine services. Many workshops suggest intervals roughly every 5,000–10,000 km for conventional oil in city driving, but the exact interval depends on oil grade, engine type, and manufacturer guidance. Used buyers should ask when the last oil change was done and whether records exist. This is general educational guidance, not a service recommendation for a specific car.

## Brake system maintenance

Brake pads and discs wear with use. Squealing, longer stopping distances, or a soft pedal are reasons to have brakes inspected by a qualified mechanic. Brake fluid also ages and may need periodic checking or replacement according to the handbook. Never assume brakes are fine without inspection — especially on high-mileage used cars.

## Timing belt and major engine service

Some petrol engines use a rubber timing belt that must be replaced at a manufacturer interval (often between roughly 60,000–100,000 km depending on engine). Timing chain engines follow different rules. A failed timing belt can cause serious engine damage on interference engines. Because the correct interval is engine-specific, verify the exact variant with a workshop or handbook — do not rely on generic mileage guesses alone.

## Transmission and clutch care

Automatic transmissions may need fluid level or condition checks at major service milestones. Manual cars can need clutch adjustment or replacement as mileage increases — slipping, shudder, or high engagement point are warning signs. CVT-equipped cars have their own service notes in the handbook. Transmission work should be diagnosed by a qualified mechanic.

## Coolant, battery, and rubber components

Coolant protects against overheating and corrosion; level and hose condition should be checked periodically, especially before summer. Batteries typically weaken after several years in hot climates. Rubber belts, hoses, and bushings age even on lower-mileage cars. Age and environment matter alongside odometer readings.
